# Consumables Management

## Overview

Consumables include toner cartridges, ink cartridges, drum units, waste toner containers, maintenance kits, and paper. Proper management helps maintain print quality, reduces the risk of unexpected downtime, and extends the useful life of each component.

---

## Consumable Lifecycle

```
Order → Receive → Store → Install → Monitor → Replace → Dispose
  │                                              │
  └────────────── Reorder point ──────────────────┘
```

### Reorder Triggers

| Consumable | Reorder When | Recommended Stock |
|------------|--------------|-------------------|
| Toner / ink cartridge | Level drops below 20% | 1 spare per printer model |
| Drum unit | Life remaining below 15% | 1 spare for high-volume printers |
| Waste toner container | 80% full indicator | 1 spare |
| Maintenance kit | 80% of rated page count | Schedule service visit |
| Paper | Below 2 weeks of supply | 1 month supply |

## Toner Cartridge Replacement

### When to Replace

- The control panel displays "Toner Low" or "Toner Empty"
- Printouts appear faded or show light vertical streaks
- The toner LED is lit amber

### Procedure (Laser Printer)

1. Prepare the replacement cartridge and keep it ready for installation. Shake it gently 5–6 times from side to side to distribute the toner evenly.
2. Ensure the printer is powered on so the cartridge can seat correctly.
3. Open the front cover fully.
4. Remove the old cartridge by gripping the handle and pulling it straight out.
5. Prepare the new cartridge by removing the protective seal along the indicated tab. Do not touch the drum surface.
6. Insert the new cartridge, aligning it with the rails and pushing firmly until it clicks into place.
7. Close the front cover. The printer will run a calibration cycle for 15–60 seconds.
8. Print a test page to confirm print quality.
9. Dispose of the old cartridge through the manufacturer's recycling program.

!!! tip "Extend toner life"
    When the "Toner Low" message first appears, remove the cartridge, shake it gently side to side five times, and reinstall it. This can yield an additional 50–200 pages and provides time to arrange a replacement.

### Toner Storage

- Store in a cool, dry place (10–30 °C, 30–70% RH)
- Keep in the original packaging until ready to install
- Do not store near heat sources or in direct sunlight
- Avoid high humidity, because toner absorbs moisture and can clump
- Typical shelf life is 2–3 years from the manufacture date; check the packaging for details

## Drum Unit Replacement

### What Is a Drum?

The drum is the photosensitive cylinder that transfers the toner image onto the paper. It is a wear item with a rated lifespan of approximately 12,000–60,000 pages, depending on the model.

### When to Replace

- The control panel displays "Drum Life End" or "Replace Drum"
- Printouts show repeating spots or smudges at regular intervals; the interval typically matches the drum circumference
- Faded or ghost images persist even after installing a new toner cartridge

### Procedure

1. Power on the printer and open the front cover.
2. Remove the toner cartridge if it is attached to the drum unit.
3. Pull the drum unit assembly straight out to remove it.
4. Unpack the new drum unit and remove all protective covers.
5. Insert the new drum unit until it clicks into place.
6. Reinstall the toner cartridge into the new drum unit.
7. Close the front cover.
8. Reset the drum counter. This step is critical; the printer will not recognize the new drum unless the counter is reset from the control panel menu.
9. Print a test page.

!!! warning "Reset the counter"
    Forgetting to reset the drum counter is the most common mistake. The printer will continue to display "Replace Drum" and may stop printing. Refer to the device's service manual for the exact reset procedure.

## Waste Toner Container

### When to Replace

- The display shows "Waste Toner Full" or "Waste Toner Box Near Full"
- Print quality declines because of toner buildup

### Procedure

1. Power off and unplug the printer.
2. Open the front or side access cover, depending on the printer model.
3. Carefully pull out the waste toner container while keeping it level to avoid spills.
4. Seal the old container in a plastic bag.
5. Install the new container and push until it clicks into place.
6. Close the cover, plug the printer back in, and power it on.
7. Reset the waste toner counter if prompted.

## Ink Cartridge Replacement (Inkjet Printers)

### When to Replace

- The display shows the ink level is low or empty
- Printouts have missing colors or horizontal white lines
- The ink droplet icon is lit

### Procedure

1. Power on the printer.
2. Open the ink cartridge access door so the carriage moves to the center.
3. Wait for the carriage to stop moving.
4. Press down on the empty cartridge to release it, then pull it out.
5. Remove the new cartridge from its packaging and peel off the protective tape covering the nozzles.
    - Do not touch the copper contacts or the nozzle plate.
6. Insert the new cartridge into the correct slot, matching the color labels, and push it up until it clicks.
7. Close the access door.
8. The printer will run an automatic priming cycle.
9. Print a nozzle check pattern to verify that all colors are firing correctly.
10. If lines are missing, run the head cleaning utility for 1–2 cycles, then perform a deep clean if necessary.

## Paper Selection and Storage

### Recommended Paper Specifications

| Attribute | Recommendation |
|-----------|----------------|
| Weight | 75–90 g/m² (20–24 lb) for general use |
| Brightness | 90+ ISO for crisp text |
| Moisture content | 4–6% (too dry = static and jams; too damp = curl and wrinkles) |
| Finish | Smooth, uncoated paper for laser printers |
| Sizes | A4, Letter, Legal (check printer specifications) |

### Paper Storage Best Practices

- Store paper in its original ream wrapper until use
- Keep paper flat on a shelf rather than on the floor, because floor moisture can cause curling
- Maintain 30–50% relative humidity in the storage area
- Do not open multiple reams at once
- Fan the paper stack before loading to separate sheets and reduce static
- For long-term storage, keep paper in a sealed cabinet with desiccant

### Paper Types to Avoid

- Paper that has already been printed on, unless using manual feed for a specific media type
- Heavily textured or rough paper, which can damage the rollers
- Paper with clips, staples, or adhesive notes
- Damp or wrinkled paper
- Labels or transparencies that are not rated for laser printers, because they can melt in the fuser

!!! danger "Laser vs. Inkjet media"
    Never use inkjet-specific transparency film or labels in a laser printer. The heat can melt the adhesive and cause severe jams. Always use media rated for the correct printer type.

## Maintenance Kit

A maintenance kit typically includes:

- Fuser unit
- Transfer roller
- Pickup rollers for all trays
- Separation pads
- Feed rollers

### Replacement Interval

Maintenance kits are typically replaced every 100,000–200,000 pages, depending on the printer model. Check the kit life counter in the printer configuration page.

!!! warning "Professional installation recommended"
    Installing a maintenance kit often requires disassembling multiple parts of the printer. Schedule replacement with an authorized service provider to avoid damage and preserve warranty coverage.

## Disposal and Recycling

| Item | Disposal Method |
|------|-----------------|
| Toner / ink cartridges | Use the manufacturer take-back program (HP, Canon, Brother, and others typically offer free recycling) |
| Waste toner | Seal in a plastic bag and dispose of it as electronic waste; do not place it in regular trash |
| Drum units | Manufacturer recycling or an e-waste facility |
| Old paper | Recycle through a standard paper recycling stream |
| Packaging | Cardboard and plastic recycling |
